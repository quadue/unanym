A fictional example

One approval. Two independent websites.

Robin belongs to an Amsterdam cohort. An international workshop website and a Berlin cohort’s resource page are run by different people. Both agree to recognise Amsterdam’s members.

  1. 1

    Amsterdam approves

    The cohort’s organiser records Robin’s membership once.

  2. 2

    Robin chooses

    She uses “Robin” on the workshop site and “R.” in Berlin, choosing separately to share her Amsterdam membership. Email and other memberships stay out.

  3. 3

    Both sites recognise it

    Each website checks that approval and opens its member page, without maintaining a separate membership approval list.

  4. 4

    Approval ends

    Amsterdam withdraws approval. Both sites close their protected pages on Robin’s next checked visit.

Each website decides whose approvals it accepts and what they open. Sites keep their own accounts. Earlier copies cannot be recalled.
