Show you belong.
Not who you are elsewhere.

Members of a community sign in to its websites with a name they choose for each one. A website learns only the memberships you decide to show it, not your email address or the other websites you use.

What a website receives

Receives

  • A name you chose for it. A first name or pseudonym is enough.
  • An identifier used by this website only. Another website gets a different one.
  • The memberships you tick, each signed and naming who approved it.

Does not receive

  • Your email address.
  • Memberships you left unticked.
  • An identifier it could match with another website. Using the same name everywhere is your choice.

Your identity service operator holds your records and can see which websites you connect. Choose an operator your community trusts, or run your own. How your information is handled →

How it works

  1. An organisation approvesAn authorised organiser records who belongs, and can withdraw it at any time.
  2. You chooseA name for each website, and which memberships it may see.
  3. The website decidesIt receives what you chose and applies its own rules for who gets in.
  4. You can disconnectStop new sharing whenever you like. Copies a website already received may remain.

How Unanym is built

Each community keeps its own rules

Organisations decide who belongs. Websites decide what a membership opens. Unanym records and passes on those decisions. It never decides who belongs.

Run by your community

Any community can run its own service with its own domain and operator. There is no central Unanym account.

Open standards

Websites connect with OpenID Connect, the protocol behind most “Sign in with…” buttons. The interface is small and published.

Open source

Apache License 2.0. The source repository is not public yet.
